Tong’s Recommended Reading

Here is a list of recommended reading for all data professionals, organized by topic.

General scientific and statistical literacy

G. E. P. Box, 1976: Science and statistics. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 71: 791-799.

G. E. P. Box, 2001: Statistics for discovery. Journal of Applied Statistics, 28: 285-299.

R. P. Feynman, 1967: The Character of Physical Law. MIT Press.

R. P. Feynman, 1998: The Meaning of it All: Thoughts of a Citizen-Scientist. Perseus Books.

John Kay and Mervyn King, 2020: Radical Uncertainty: Decision-Making Beyond the Numbers (New York: W. W. Norton).

C. Krumme, 2017: Babylonian lottery. Edge.

G. J. Lithgow, M. Driscoll, and P. Phillips, 2017: A long journey to reproducible results. Nature, 548: 387-388.

J. J. Locascio, 2019: The impact of results blind scientific publishing on statistical consultation and collaboration. The American Statistician, 73 (sup1): 346-351.

C. Mallows, 1998: The zeroth problem. The American Statistician, 52: 1-9.

N. Oreskes, K. Shrader-Frechette, and K. Belitz, 1994: Verification, validation, and confirmation of numerical models in the earth sciences. Science, 263: 641-646.

H. Quinn, 2009: What is science? Physics Today, 62 (7): 8-9.

M. Schrage, 2000: How the bell curve cheats you. Fortune, 21 February 2000, p. 296.

L. Shepp, 2007: Statistical thinking: From Tukey to Vardi and beyond. In Complex Datasets and Inverse Problems: Tomography, Networks and Beyond, ed. by R. Liu, W. Strawderman, and C.-H. Zhang. IMS Lecture Notes-Monograph Series, 54: 268-273.

E. L. Thompson and L. A. Smith, 2019: Escape from model-land. Economics, 13: 2019-40.

J. W. Tukey, 1969: Analyzing data: sanctification or detective work? American Psychologist, 24: 83-91.

Exploratory and confirmatory

P. Diaconis, 1985: Theories of data analysis: From magical thinking through classical statistics. In Exploring Data Tables, Trends, and Shapes, ed. by D. C. Hoaglin, F. Mosteller, and J. W. Tukey (New York: Wiley), 1-36.

J. S. Mogil and M. R. Macleod, 2017: No publication without confirmation. Nature, 542: 409-411.

J. W. Tukey, 1980: We need both exploratory and confirmatory. American Statistician, 34: 23-25.

Probability

W. E. Deming, 1975: On probability as a basis for action. The American Statistician, 29: 146-152.

W. Feller, 1970: Introduction. An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications, revised third edition (New York: Wiley), Chapter 1 (pp. 1-6).

Statistical inference

V. Amrhein, D. Trafimow, and S. Greenland, 2019: Inferential statistics as descriptive statistics: there is no replication crisis if we don’t expect replication. The American Statistician, 73 (sup1): 262-270.

J. O. Berger and D. A. Berry, 1988: Statistical analysis and the illusion of objectivity. American Scientist, 76: 159-165.

G. Gigerenzer and J. Marewski, 2015: Surrogate science: The idol of a universal method for scientific inference. Journal of Management, 41: 421-440.

S. Greenland, 2025: Statistical methods: basic concepts, interpretations, and cautions. Handbook of Epidemiology, ed. by W. Ahrens and I. Pigeot (New York: Springer). ArXiv version.

B. Gunter and C. Tong, 2017: What are the odds!? The “airport fallacy” and statistical inference. Significance, 14 (4): 38-41.

J. R. Hollenbeck and P. M. Wright, 2016: Harking, sharking, and tharking: Making the case for post hoc analysis of scientific data. Journal of Management, 43: 5-18.

R. L. Wasserstein and N. A. Lazar, 2016: The ASA’s statement on p-values: context, process, and purpose. The American Statistician, 70: 129-133.

Design of experiments

R. A. Fisher, 1926: The arrangement of field experiments. Journal of the Ministry of Agriculture of Great Britain, 33: 503-513.

International Conference on Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use, 2022: E8(R1) General Considerations for Clinical Studies.

Observational data

A. Bradford Hill, 1965: The environment and disease: association or causation? Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine, 58 (5): 295-300.

D. A. Freedman, 1999: From association to causation: some remarks on the history of statistics. Statistical Science, 14: 243-258.

Model uncertainty and the garden of forking paths

C. Chatfield, 1995: Model uncertainty, data mining and statistical inference (with discussion). Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series A, 158: 419-466.

A. Gelman and E. Loken, 2014: The statistical crisis in science. American Scientist, 102: 460-465.

J. P. Simmons, L. D. Nelson, and U. Simonsohn, 2011: False-positive psychology: undisclosed flexibility in data collection and analysis allows presenting anything as significant. Psychological Science, 22: 1359-1366.

Many sets of data; many lines of evidence

C. Cotton, 2026: Rigor is not readiness: the PERL framework for evidence-based policy. Science, 393 (6810): 463-465.

A. S. C. Ehrenberg, 1990: A hope for the future of statistics: MSOD. The American Statistician, 44: 195-196.

A. S. C. Ehrenberg and J. A. Bound, 1993: Predictability and prediction (with discussion). Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series A, 156: 167-206.

M. R. Munafo and G. Davey Smith, 2018: Robust research needs many lines of evidence. Nature, 553: 399-401.

Interpretation of statistical results

D. G. Altman and J. M. Bland, 1995: Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. British Medical Journal, 311: 485.

S. Greenland, 2017: The need for cognitive science in methodology. American Journal of Epidemiology, 186: 639-645.

M. Lavine, 2019: Frequentist, Bayes, or other? The American Statistican, 73 (sup1): 312-318.

B. B. McShane, D. Gal, A. Gelman, C. Robert, and J. L. Tackett, 2019: Abandon statistical significance. The American Statistician, 73 (sup1): 235-245.

Simulation and random numbers

B. Hayes, 2001: Randomness as a resource. American Scientist, 89: 300-304.

T. Speed, 2012: Does it work in practice? IMS Bulletin, 41 (8): 9.

Statistics and machine learning, data mining, and data science

L. Breiman, 2001: Statistical modeling: The two cultures (with discussion). Statistical Science, 16: 199-231.

D. Donoho, 2017: 50 years of data science (with discussion, not linked here). Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics, 26: 745-785.

J. H. Friedman, 2001: The role of statistics in the data revolution? International Statistical Review, 69: 5-10.

Statistical consulting

D. R. Cox, 1999: Some general remarks on consulting. Liaison, 13 (1).

S. Evans, 2026: Statistics is a journey, not a destination. Amstat News, issue 585 (March, 2026), pp. 35-36. (Or see earlier version published in the Biopharmaceutical Report in 2025).

R. W. Hamming, 1973: The art of computing for scientists and engineers. Numerical Methods for Scientists and Engineers, second edition (New York: McGraw-Hill; reprinted by Dover in 1986), Chapter “N+1” (pp. 702-710).

R. Nisbet, J. Elder, and G. Miner, 2009: Top 10 data mining mistakes. Handbook of Statistical Analysis and Data Mining Applications, first edition. Academic Press, Chapter 20 (pp. 733-754).

Statistics in preclinical research

J. Couzin-Frankel, 2013: When mice mislead. Science, 342: 922-925.

S. Landis, et al., 2012: A call for transparent reporting to optimize the predictive value of preclinical research. Nature, 490: 187-191.

P. S. Reynolds, 2022: Between two stools: preclinical research, reproducibility, and statistical design of experiments. BMC Research Notes, 15: 73.

Statistics in experimental physics

E. Cartlidge, 2021: Solving the proton puzzle. Physics World, June 2021 issue.

M. Jeng, 2006: A selected history of expectation bias in physics. American Journal of Physics, 74: 578-583,

D. Kleppner, 1992: Fretting about statistics. Physics Today, vol. 45, no. 7 (July 1992), pp. 9-11.

R. Matthews, 2024: The OPERA experiment versus Einstein. Significance, July 2024, pp. 42-43.

C. Seife, 2000: CERN’s gamble shows perils, rewards of playing the odds. Science, 289: 2260-2262.

W. J. Youden, 1961: Systematic errors in physical constants. Physics Today, 14 (9): 32-43.

W. J. Youden, 1972: Enduring values. Technometrics, 14 (1): 1-11.

Some helpful online resources

  • American Statistical Association, Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice
  • American Statistical Association, What to Expect When You Consult a Statistician
  • Nature Portfolio collection on Statistics for Biologists
  • PHYSTAT, an event series for statistical methods in high energy physics
  • NIST/Sematech e-Handbook of Statistical Methods
